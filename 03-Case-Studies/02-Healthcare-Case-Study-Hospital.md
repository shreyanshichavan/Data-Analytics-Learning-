# Case Study 02: Healthcare – Hospital Patient Analytics

## Introduction

Healthcare organizations generate a large amount of data every day.

This data can come from patient registrations, appointments, treatments, 
diagnostic tests, hospital departments, billing, and patient feedback.

A Data Analyst helps the hospital understand this data and use it to 
improve healthcare services and hospital operations.

---

## Business Situation

Suppose a hospital wants to understand its patient activity and improve 
the overall patient experience.

The management wants answers to questions such as:

- How many patients visit the hospital every month?
- Which department receives the most patients?
- Which diseases or health conditions are more common?
- What is the average waiting time?
- How many appointments are cancelled?
- Which doctors have the highest number of appointments?
- How satisfied are patients with the hospital services?

A Data Analyst can study the available data and provide useful insights 
to management.

---

## What Data Can a Hospital Have?

A hospital may have different types of data, such as:

- Patient information
- Patient age and gender
- Appointment records
- Doctor information
- Department information
- Diagnosis information
- Treatment records
- Test results
- Admission and discharge dates
- Billing information
- Patient feedback
- Appointment cancellation records

---

## Role of a Data Analyst

The Data Analyst studies hospital data to understand patient behaviour 
and hospital performance.

### 1. Patient Volume Analysis

The analyst can calculate the number of patients visiting the hospital 
daily, weekly, or monthly.

This helps the hospital understand patient demand and plan resources.

### 2. Department Performance

The analyst can compare different departments such as:

- Cardiology
- Orthopedics
- Neurology
- Pediatrics
- General Medicine

This helps management understand which departments have higher patient 
demand.

### 3. Appointment Analysis

The analyst can study appointment data to understand:

- Number of appointments
- Completed appointments
- Cancelled appointments
- Missed appointments
- Peak appointment hours

This can help improve appointment scheduling.

### 4. Waiting Time Analysis

A Data Analyst can analyze the time patients spend waiting before 
consultation.

If a particular department has a consistently high waiting time, 
management can investigate the reason.

### 5. Patient Feedback Analysis

Patient feedback can be analyzed to understand satisfaction levels.

The analyst can identify common complaints and areas where hospital 
services can be improved.

---

## Important KPIs

Some useful healthcare KPIs include:

- Total Patients
- New Patients
- Returning Patients
- Average Waiting Time
- Appointment Cancellation Rate
- Patient Satisfaction Score
- Average Length of Stay
- Department-wise Patient Count
- Doctor-wise Appointment Count
- Bed Occupancy Rate

---

## Example Business Question

Suppose the hospital notices that patients are waiting for a long time 
in the outpatient department.

The Data Analyst can investigate:

1. Which department has the highest waiting time?
2. What time of day has the highest patient volume?
3. How many doctors are available during peak hours?
4. How many appointments are scheduled at the same time?
5. Is the waiting time higher on particular days?

---

## Possible Insight

Suppose the analysis shows that patient waiting time is highest between 
10 AM and 1 PM because patient volume is much higher during this period.

The hospital can consider increasing doctor availability or improving 
appointment scheduling during peak hours.

This is an example of how data can support an operational decision.

---

## How Data Analytics Helps Healthcare

Data Analytics can help hospitals to:

- Improve patient experience
- Reduce waiting time
- Improve resource planning
- Understand patient demand
- Improve department performance
- Identify operational problems
- Support better decision-making
- Improve overall hospital efficiency

---

## Conclusion

A Data Analyst in healthcare works with patient, appointment, treatment, 
and operational data to identify useful patterns and problems.

The goal is not only to create reports but also to provide insights that 
can help healthcare organizations improve their services and make 
better decisions.

The basic flow is:

Data → Analysis → Insights → Decision → Improvement
