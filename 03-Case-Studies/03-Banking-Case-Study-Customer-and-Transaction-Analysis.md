# Case Study 03: Banking – Customer and Transaction Analysis

## Introduction

Banks handle a large amount of data every day.

This includes customer information, bank accounts, transactions, loans, 
credit cards, digital payments, and customer service records.

A Data Analyst helps banks understand customer behaviour, monitor 
transactions, measure business performance, and identify areas that 
require attention.

---

## Business Situation

Suppose a bank wants to understand how customers are using its services.

The management wants answers to questions such as:

- How many active customers does the bank have?
- Which type of account is most popular?
- How frequently do customers make transactions?
- Which customers are using digital banking?
- Which branches have the highest transaction volume?
- How many customers are applying for loans?
- Why are some customers leaving the bank?

A Data Analyst can analyze this information and provide useful insights.

---

## What Data Can a Bank Have?

A bank may work with data such as:

- Customer information
- Account details
- Transaction records
- Deposit and withdrawal data
- Loan applications
- Credit card transactions
- Digital banking activity
- Branch information
- Customer complaints
- Customer feedback

---

## Role of a Data Analyst

### 1. Customer Analysis

The analyst can divide customers into different groups based on their 
behaviour, account type, transaction frequency, or other business 
characteristics.

This helps the bank understand its customers better.

### 2. Transaction Analysis

The analyst can study transaction data to understand:

- Transaction volume
- Transaction value
- Frequently used services
- Peak transaction periods
- Branch-wise transactions
- Digital versus branch transactions

### 3. Loan Analysis

A Data Analyst can analyze loan applications and understand:

- Number of applications
- Approved loans
- Rejected loans
- Loan amount
- Repayment behaviour
- Customer segments applying for loans

This can help the bank understand its lending business.

### 4. Customer Churn Analysis

The analyst can identify customers who have stopped using banking 
services or are likely to leave.

The bank can then investigate possible reasons and improve customer 
retention.

### 5. Digital Banking Analysis

Banks can analyze how customers use:

- Mobile banking
- Internet banking
- UPI
- ATM services
- Online payments

This helps the bank understand the adoption of digital services.

---

## Important KPIs

Some important banking KPIs include:

- Total Customers
- Active Customers
- New Customers
- Customer Retention Rate
- Customer Churn Rate
- Transaction Volume
- Total Transaction Value
- Loan Approval Rate
- Loan Default Rate
- Digital Banking Adoption Rate

---

## Example Business Question

Suppose a bank notices that many customers are opening accounts but 
becoming inactive after a few months.

The Data Analyst can investigate:

1. Which customer groups become inactive?
2. How frequently were these customers using the bank?
3. Which services were they using?
4. Did they use digital banking?
5. Which branches have higher customer churn?
6. Are there common patterns among inactive customers?

---

## Possible Insight

Suppose the analysis shows that customers who do not use digital banking 
are more likely to become inactive.

The bank may decide to improve digital banking awareness and provide 
customers with better guidance on using mobile and online banking 
services.

---

## How Data Analytics Helps Banking

Data Analytics helps banks to:

- Understand customer behaviour
- Improve customer experience
- Monitor business performance
- Improve digital banking services
- Analyze loan performance
- Reduce customer churn
- Identify business opportunities
- Support better decision-making

---

## Conclusion

Data Analytics plays an important role in the banking industry.

A Data Analyst converts customer and transaction data into meaningful 
insights that help banks improve services, understand customers, and 
make better business decisions.

The basic flow is:

Data → Analysis → Insights → Business Decision → Improvement
